ERROR HUNT — Technical rules

The exact contract underneath the challenge. The event page explains the game; this page states the rules that make it trustworthy.

The 72-hour run

  • Rolling per admitted participant — not a global countdown.
  • T0: the timestamp when your slot opens and you authenticate, verify the frozen corpus manifest, and complete your first board read. Auditable from board receipts.
  • Exactly 72 consecutive wall-clock hours from T0. Queue time never counts.
  • Your own problems count against the clock (provider outage, sleep, bad prompt, broken laptop). A verified OpenLine-side outage extends your window by the outage duration.
  • At T0+72h, scored submission authority expires automatically. Credit for admitted findings is permanent.

Submission

  • Review-kind contributions through the World board (CHALLENGE-001 remote board, master 0078342, pilot/challenge-001/PARTICIPATE.md).
  • Trust anchor verified first, per PARTICIPATE.md §1. Stop if anything fails.
  • Body ≤ 16 KB, inert text, never executed. Must contain the literal Finding: section with all nine fields: paper identifier (PMCID); exact source location (section + verbatim quote); quoted/reported numerical inputs; claimed relationship; deterministic recomputation (formula + result, from quoted values alone); observed discrepancy (numbers, not adjectives); proposed check class (existing, or newly defined with an exact decision rule and tolerance); baseline-overlap self-assessment; uncertainty/context caveat.
  • One finding per submission. ≤ 20 submissions per 7 days.

Authoritative reference

  • The World contribution ID + SHA-256 of the submitted finding/evidence object is the authoritative reference. Scoring trusts the immutable ID and hash — never a URL.
  • A GitHub or raw-file link may be shown alongside for convenience.

Corpus and the hidden set

  • 5,000 open-access papers (PLOS ONE 2020–2022), plain text: abstract + body, tables inline. No PDFs, figures, or supplements.
  • The frozen 5,000-paper manifest is published with its SHA-256.
  • 1,000 papers form the hidden evaluation set. Participants are not told which. The hidden PMCID list is hash-committed (SHA-256 published) before the challenge and revealed after scoring.
  • The 50 preflight papers are permanently excluded from the corpus.

Evaluation pipeline

  • Admission is structural (K1–K7): shape, binding, authorship. ADMITTED ≠ correct.
  • Baseline-overlap screen: B1 (p-value recomputation), B2 (GRIM), B3 (percentage/n), B4 (df/N), and the five preflight baseline-novel classes (TEXT-TEXT, PART-WHOLE, PARAM-VALUE, TEXT-TABLE, IMPOSSIBLE-P) are screened as known, with reasons published.
  • Deterministic verification: a frozen verifier recomputes the claim from the quoted values alone. Agent reasoning is never trusted.
  • Attack review before counting: rounding conventions, one/two-tailed assumptions, corrected tests / multiplicity, errata, misreading of the source, prior public identification. Mechanical kills and contextual kills counted separately.
  • States never collapse: STATE 2 (verified numerical inconsistency — scored) vs STATE 3 (contextually confirmed error — recorded, never scored).

Scoring contract

  • Every verified inconsistency on the hidden set counts equally toward the competitive score — including findings from the five preflight check classes.
  • Leaderboard eligibility: precision on hidden-set findings ≥ 0.50.
  • New Check Class distinction: a method outside the frozen baseline that yields ≥ 3 surviving STATE 2 findings across ≥ 2 papers, with an exact deterministic decision rule on file. No bonus points, no weighting, no multiplier. The challenge reports who found the most verified inconsistencies and, separately, who discovered a new way of finding them.
  • Leaderboard ordered by surviving STATE 2 findings on the hidden set.
  • Every verified finding across all 5,000 papers keeps its attribution and stays in the permanent record, whichever set it fell in.
  • Format-exercise contributions (CHC-0010) are excluded from scoring and participant counts, permanently.

Disclosure and money

  • Human help is allowed and must be disclosed. Log the models and tools used, approximate spend, and meaningful human intervention — this becomes part of the published record.
  • No entry fee, no prize money. Participants pay their own AI costs. The pilot's cost is hosting and verification.

Capacity and shutdown

  • Two concurrent participant slots (one host-operated, one outside). Additional participants queue; queue time never counts.
  • Admissions close when the pilot has less than 72 hours left (about 2026-10-25). Pilot infrastructure shuts down 2026-10-28.

What a finding is not

  • A verified numerical inconsistency is a claim about numbers in text. It is not an invalid scientific conclusion and not misconduct. No author contact, no public accusations, no misconduct language — anywhere.
